A1 × B3 — grid-first shell meets saved views

You picked A1 (slim bar + segmented module tabs, grid at 90%+) and B3 (Airtable/Notion: saved views as tabs, a collapsible nav drawer, and a scrollable record panel). These five iterations fuse them: every one keeps A1's minimal header + edge-to-edge grid and swaps the floating popover for B3's scrollable record panel — they differ only in how modules, saved views, and the panel compose. Same real seed cohort + persona ($500 · 28″ · owns Wayfinder).

Kept from A1: hamburger + minimal header, segmented module tabs, frozen option column, sticky head, limit chips
Kept from B3: saved views (Under budget, Fits my car…), collapsible workspace drawer, row → scrollable record panel
The question: one tab row or two? views as tabs or chips? panel as side-sheet or drawer-driven?
Constant: the comparison grid is always the page — chrome earns its pixels or it's gone

Five iterations on the chosen direction

C4Views live in the drawer; tabs stay modules-only

Keep the strip minimal (modules only) and move saved views into the hamburger drawer, B3's workspace tree style: Tables = modules, nested Views below each. The drawer is closed by default (grid full width); here it's open to show the tree. Best when parents accumulate many views.

Where I'd land

Ship C1 (two-tier module tabs over saved views) as the structure, with C3's scrollable side-panel as the row interaction and C5's density + group-by-fit as optional power toggles. Start saved views seeded with All, Under budget, and Fits my car so tier 2 is useful on day one. C4's drawer tree is the graceful overflow once a parent saves more views than the strip can hold.
