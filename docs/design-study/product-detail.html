Product detail: a grid of values, not opaque scores

Six mid-fidelity directions for the product-detail surface, plus five analogies from adjacent problem spaces. Every mockup below is rendered from the real seed data — 15 infant car seats, their actual specs, weights and 0–5 scores — and anchored to one concrete parent so “compared to what?” always has an answer. Philosophically Tufte: show the measurement, enforce comparison within the eyespan, let any score ride as a thin top layer that decomposes back into its parts.

Part 1 — Six proposed interfaces

1. Anchored Value Rows

Each requirement leads with its literal measurement; a strip places that value on the cohort’s full range, tints the “good” end, plots the other 14 seats as ghost dots, and marks your threshold (back-seat 28″, budget $500). The 0–5 score survives only as a faint trailing tag.

4. Fit Ledger — “vs. your life”

5. Per-Criterion Dot-Plots

The purest Tufte move: one horizontal strip per spec, all 15 seats plotted as dots by their true value, this product highlighted and labelled, the persona’s threshold marked. Reading the focal dot down the stack is the product detail — and you see the full distribution and clustering for free.

6. Decomposed Contribution Bar

Keeps a single weighted total — because the score does encode the creator’s preference weighting that raw specs can’t — but never as a bare number. Each segment is one criterion’s weight×score contribution, hover reveals the underlying value, and stacking two products compares where the points come from, not just the totals.

Recommendation

Lead the product-detail page with Concept 1 (Anchored Value Rows) — it honors your “detail is the most important surface” brief while smuggling the comparison into every row, so no value is ever opaque. Pair it with a compact Concept 4 (Fit Ledger) block up top to cash in the persona data you already collect, and offer Concept 2 (Matrix) as the “compare all” view one tap away. Keep Concept 6 as the only place a rolled-up score appears, fully decomposed. Concepts 3 & 5 are strong but better as progressive-disclosure flourishes.

The through-line, straight from Tufte: show the measurement, anchor it (to the cohort and to the parent), and let the score be a thin, decomposable top layer — never the headline.
